# LyricQ Hint Guidelines

## The Rules

### 1. Speak in the title's language, but talk about Frank's meaning — and land the ending.
Use words that naturally belong to the title's literal world (plant words for Ivy, sky words for Pink + White, numbness words for Novacane), but apply them to describe what's actually happening emotionally in the song. The ending of the hint matters most — don't trail off into something vague or clunky. Finish with something that hits emotionally and stays in the title's metaphor.

**Example (Ivy):**
- Bad: "Frank named this after something that grows on you until it's part of the structure — beautiful, but try removing it" *(describes the plant, not the song — and the ending is vague)*
- Bad: "...he couldn't pull it out without tearing something" *(vague, loses the metaphor at the end)*
- Good: "Frank titled this after how a first love grew into him so deep he couldn't pull it out without taking a piece of him with it" *(plant language — "grew," "pull out" — but describing the relationship, and the ending lands)*

### 2. It's about Frank's choice, not a dictionary definition.
Every hint should answer: *why did Frank pick this word as the title?* Don't describe what the literal word means — describe what Frank is saying *through* it.

### 3. One complete thought.
No em-dashes splitting the hint into two separate ideas. The whole hint should flow as a single sentence. If the idea before the dash makes sense on its own, the second half is a separate thought and shouldn't be there.

### 4. Point toward the title without saying it.
The hint should make you think "oh, that must be ___" without using the actual title word.

### 5. Don't lean on trivia.
No facts about misspellings, brand logos, Greek mythology origins, or wordplay about the title itself. Those are fun facts, not hints about how Frank uses the concept.
